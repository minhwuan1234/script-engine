# Hook quality

A hook begins the script's narrative promise; it is not a detachable teaser or a
summary of every instruction. Establish one concrete point of interest, why it is
useful to this viewer, and what the following beats will explain or demonstrate.
Relevance may come from a source-stated task or decision; it need not imply urgency.

## Choose a grounded opening

Choose a move compatible with the assigned style and supported by the document:

- Decision moment: introduce a stated action and its condition. An illustrative moment can make it concrete; do not assume what the viewer
  would do or invent a dangerous wrong action.
- Mystery or partial view: connect source-provided evidence to its stated
  explanation. Do not hide a trivial label or delay an urgent instruction.
- Unexpected viewpoint: an object or system can guide a clearly illustrative
  explanation when the source supplies its function. Personification is a voice
  choice, not permission to invent motives, agency, mechanisms or outcomes. Avoid
  trivializing suffering; omit this device when it conflicts with the assigned style.
- Contrast: place explicit source facts side by side to clarify a distinction.
  Do not invent a widespread belief or imply causation the source does not establish.

Open with concrete, source-specific language. A question is optional. Do not force
multiple questions into the hook or invent factual stakes to manufacture suspense.
A witty cold open, an expressive object or a visual surprise may establish the
promise under the F.Learning voice guidance; the factual premise stays grounded.
Give a useful takeaway directly when appropriate; leave its application or
explanation to the body instead of repeating the whole script twice.

## Length and payoff

Give the opening enough room to establish its point of interest and promise. The
maximum is 15 seconds for every video length. There is no separate hook word limit
or word quota; do not compress the idea to meet an arbitrary word count. Use natural
spoken pacing, not rushed delivery to fit the time limit. Choose an actual end
timestamp that fits the spoken words and meaningful visual beats. Start the
body immediately at that timestamp; A and B may use different hook lengths.

The next beat must advance the promise rather than restate the hook. Pay it off
through the sourced explanation, distinction or action by the end. Do not add
facts to make the payoff more dramatic. A callback is optional.

## Review before release

- Specificity: could the opening be pasted onto unrelated documents? If so, anchor
  it in a detail of this source.
- Relevance: is there a useful reason to keep watching beyond learning a label?
- Clarity: can the viewer follow the opening without assumed context?
- Payoff: do later beats fulfill the promise without replaying the opening list?
- Visual support: does the opening image help explain the same point as the voice?
- Fidelity: are all factual premises supported, including those implied by a
  question, personification or contrast?

Revise the weak opening or outline before releasing the version. Source accuracy
and urgent instructions take priority over any hook technique.
