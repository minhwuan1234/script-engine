# Healthcare / pharma / patient education

Help the viewer picture the decision, understand the source's explanation and know
what action to take. Choose the teaching emphasis that fits the document: making a
rule usable, connecting a mechanism to an action, or distinguishing stated triggers.
Use a supplied audience profile when present; the healthcare label alone does not
establish whether the viewer is a patient, caregiver or professional.

Write with warmth, specificity and a natural spoken rhythm. Clarity can be vivid:
use a moment of decision, a sustained visual motif, an illustrative conversation,
a narrow analogy or an object/system viewpoint where it helps. A calm clinical
instruction does not require flat narration throughout the script. Let the assigned
style and material determine pace and emotional register.

Illustrative scenes and hypothetical questions may be newly created under the
shared source-accuracy rules. A scene need not already exist in the leaflet. Keep
its clinical content grounded: do not introduce symptoms, treatment stages, care
settings or personal outcomes that change the instruction's meaning or scope.
Teaching hypotheses guide the story; they are not claims about patient behavior.

Keep doses, thresholds, timings, exceptions, procedural order and “call when” rules
intact, with every condition attached. Do not add clinical mechanisms, definitions,
risks, reassurance or provider relationships absent from the source. If the “why”
is missing, make the action understandable without inventing a medical explanation.
Preserve dignity and avoid blame or shame. A callback can resolve the opening's
question or image while reinforcing the actual sourced action.
