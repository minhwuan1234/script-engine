# Healthcare / pharma / patient education

- Typical documents: discharge instructions, condition leaflets, medication guides,
  pre-operation preparation and healthcare-professional education.
- Aim for calm, direct clarity that helps the viewer perform the source-stated action.
- Make the main job a decision or action a viewer can perform. Show the exact
  source-stated trigger, action and timing together, so the rule can be used at the
  moment it matters. Do not turn the document into a section-by-section summary.
- Use one supported understanding need from the materials sheet to guide the
  explanation. If the source and audience brief do not establish a mistaken belief,
  setting or behavior, do not invent one for a hook or scene.
- When the assigned style is a first-person journey, keep it to one decision moment.
  Include a patient, place, day, symptom or thought only when explicitly supplied.
  Otherwise use a neutral first-person action sequence. When the assigned style is
  Q&A, ask only questions the source can answer. When it is Myth-buster, correct only
  a belief the source or audience brief actually identifies or contradicts.
- Preserve clinical instructions, thresholds and `call when` wording exactly.
- Do not blame or shame people about weight, diet, medication use or adherence.
- Do not invent outcomes, prevalence, mortality, risk or reassurance.
- Mention a care team only when the source does. Do not invent a provider relationship.
- Treat medical drift and unsupported mechanisms as the highest-risk failures.
- For medication instructions, retain exact doses, timing, missed-dose rules and
  prohibitions; do not soften a `do not` to sound friendlier.
- For discharge or self-monitoring material, keep every source-stated `when to call`
  trigger attached to its action. Do not invent reassurance or urgency.
- For preparation instructions, retain the source's exact order and timing.
- Do not fill a missing `why`, definition, symptom or common mistake with general
  clinical knowledge. Flag the gap in `Engine notes` instead.
- Keep the viewer-facing wording calm and specific. End with the chosen action and
  its exact source-stated trigger; use a callback to the hook only when it adds no
  new clinical premise.
