# Before/after scenario

Let the viewer see a meaningful change in understanding or application. The contrast
can be an initial partial view and the fuller view after explanation; the PDF does
not have to contain two documented real-world cases.

Use mirrored scenes, a split screen, a replay with new context, or the same object
seen twice. Choose the order freely: show the destination first, then how to get
there, or let the contrast emerge gradually. The “after” should make a sourced
instruction more usable, not simply repeat the “before” in different words.

For example, an illustrative employee can first face an unclear instruction, then
identify the source-stated option to ask their manager. End on that action; do not
invent the manager's reply, a productivity gain or a resolved problem.

Distinguish a staged learning contrast from a claimed outcome. Improvements in
health, safety, performance or program impact need source evidence. When only one
action is given, show it first in overview and then with its actual conditions or
steps, rather than manufacturing a harmful “before” or a success story.
