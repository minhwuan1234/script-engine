# Corporate L&D / compliance / onboarding

Help the viewer recognize when and how to use the information at work. Choose the
most useful teaching emphasis: finding a next step, understanding a process,
resolving an unclear instruction or distinguishing a permission from a duty.
The ICP is an editorial lens, not a scene template or a prescribed tone.

Use a natural colleague-to-colleague voice, with energy and rhythm appropriate to
the subject. Illustrative workplace scenes, generic characters, brief dialogue,
comparisons and visual demonstrations are welcome under the shared source-accuracy
rules. A pause over an instruction can make a rule tangible without needing a real
employee case in the document. Let the assigned style shape the experience.

Preserve duties, permissions, prohibitions and exceptions exactly. A fictional role
or scene must not change who a policy applies to or create a company practice.
Do not invent deadlines, disciplinary consequences, legal requirements, productivity
results or promises about how others respond. Keep characters respectful; use the
scene to clarify the task rather than blame an employee.
