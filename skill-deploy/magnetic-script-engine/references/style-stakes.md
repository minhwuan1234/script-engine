# Stakes story

Make the relevance of a source-supported task tangible through a person facing a
choice, question or practical need. A real case is useful when supplied, but a
clearly hypothetical scene or composite illustration can also carry the story.

Choose a human-scale entry point: a pause before acting, two needs in the same
moment, or an object that makes the task concrete. Use brief dialogue, restrained
performed emotion or a return to the opening image when it helps. Let the sourced
information give the person a next step; a dramatic crisis is not required.

For example, an illustration can show someone pausing over a form before applying
the source's stated instruction. The stakes are completing that task; you need not
invent a denied benefit, a deadline or a life-changing outcome.

Mark invented scenes as illustrations when they might look like evidence. Preserve
real cases without embellishing their outcomes. Do not fabricate testimonials,
beneficiaries, impact statistics, clinical consequences or claims about how a group
feels. A hypothetical character's hesitation is staging, not evidence about the
audience. End on a supported choice or action, without forcing a success story or CTA.
