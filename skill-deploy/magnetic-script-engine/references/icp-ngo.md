# NGO / mission-driven

Make the source's purpose, process or evidence understandable at a human scale.
Choose the emphasis that fits: following how a program works, connecting a stated
need to an action, or making an impact report's distinctions clear. Let the story
carry meaning without treating every report as an appeal for donations.

Use an empathetic, specific voice. A real sourced case, a clearly hypothetical
character or a labeled composite illustration can provide a point of view. Dialogue,
visual motifs and scenes may be created under the shared source-accuracy rules.
A fictional scene can show how a stated process works; it cannot prove that the
program helped someone or that a group feels a particular way.

Keep real people and communities dignified. Distinguish illustration from testimony;
do not attribute invented quotes, suffering, events or outcomes to actual people or
organizations. Preserve evidence strength, uncertainty and the scope of impact
claims. Use only a source-supported requested action; if none is given, close with
the supported takeaway rather than invent a fundraising or advocacy instruction.
