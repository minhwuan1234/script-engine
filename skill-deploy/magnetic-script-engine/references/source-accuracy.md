# Source-only accuracy contract

Every factual statement in the title, hook, questions, voiceover and on-screen
direction must be directly supported by an explicit source passage. Do not use
medical, legal, policy, historical or general knowledge from memory, even when it is
widely accepted or correct.

## Claim rules

- Do not infer causes, mechanisms, outcomes, symptoms, timing, urgency, definitions,
  recommendations, audience characteristics or settings.
- Do not claim what people usually think, feel, misunderstand or do unless the source
  or an explicitly supplied audience brief says so.
- Do not combine two source facts into a causal, chronological or logical relationship
  unless the source explicitly connects them.
- Creative framing may change perspective, order, grouping, rhythm, phrasing and
  visual presentation. Its factual meaning must remain supported by the source.
- If a factual claim cannot be traced to an exact source passage, remove it from the script
  and report the missing information in `Engine notes`.

## Locked facts

Record these verbatim with their source locations:

- numbers, thresholds, units, timings and frequencies
- medication, dosage or procedural instructions, including every `do not`
- red-flag rules such as `call` or `go to emergency when X`
- wording the source itself marks as required

Keep every condition, qualifier, negation, population, unit, sequence, timeframe and
exception attached to its fact. Simplify the surrounding sentence, never the locked
item. Do not convert units, round values, expand abbreviations, translate terms or
normalize terminology unless the source supplies the equivalent wording.

## Missing, unclear or conflicting information

- Never choose a supposedly safer interpretation.
- Omit an instruction that cannot be used without interpretation.
- Quote the exact unclear or conflicting passages in `Engine notes` for review.
- If a useful mechanism, definition or example is absent, omit it from the script and
  name the gap in `Engine notes`.
- Factual assumptions are never allowed.

## Editorial freedom within the factual boundary

Trace factual claims to the source, not every creative word or production choice.
You may create section labels, transitions, a recurring visual motif, layouts and
animations that organize source facts. For example, a source-listed checklist can
be shown with its items appearing in sequence; the source need not specify that
animation. Do not imply a required sequence unless the source specifies one.

A generic first-person demonstration of source instructions is a presentation
choice, not a real testimonial. Do not add personal history, emotions, dialogue,
medical outcomes or evidence that the demonstration actually happened. Keep
illustrations abstract when realistic staging would imply an unsupported setting.

Questions must have source-supported premises. Do not call a newly invented belief
common, attribute it to viewers, or use a question to introduce an unsupported
claim. A contrast between two explicit source facts is available even when the
source does not label it as a contrast; do not turn it into a new causal explanation.
