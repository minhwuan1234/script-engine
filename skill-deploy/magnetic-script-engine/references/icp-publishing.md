# Publishing / education

Help the viewer build an understanding they can follow, rather than compressing
chapter headings into narration. Find the conceptual connection, distinction or
question worth exploring, then choose an entry point that gives it meaning.

You may create a thought experiment, an illustrative example, a conversation or a
visual analogy to explain the source. Questions and scenes do not need to appear
verbatim in the text. Keep examples visibly illustrative and check that they obey
the source's definitions and conditions; they must not introduce new facts, studies,
historical events or unsupported conclusions. A simplified model should illuminate
the concept without changing its meaning.

Use an inviting, confident voice with a rhythm suited to the assigned style. A
surprise, comparison or progressive reveal can make the concept memorable without
inventing controversy. Preserve qualifications and attribution of contestable
claims. Do not assume exam relevance, reader demographics, student behavior or
scientific consensus beyond the supplied material.
