# Explainer / mechanism reveal

Build the pleasure of understanding how the source's pieces fit together. Reveal
one relationship at a time and connect it to the viewer's task. A visual sequence,
close-up, cutaway, recurring object or narrowly mapped analogy can carry the
explanation while the voiceover directs attention.

Choose the route: start with a stated effect and trace its sourced cause, assemble
a process in stages, or begin with an action and explain the source's reason for it.
An object may narrate a clearly illustrative explanation if that helps the viewer;
its voice must not imply new agency, physiology or behavior.

The document need not supply the animation or metaphor. It must supply the process
or relationship being taught. If it provides actions but no causal mechanism,
reveal how the stated steps or decision conditions fit together instead, and note
the missing “why”. Preserve an explanatory progression rather than reverting to a
list. Do not fill a missing mechanism with outside knowledge.
