# ICP lenses

One engine; the lens changes who the viewer is, what "magnetic" means, and which guardrails apply. Infer the ICP from the doc (vocabulary, audience, author) and state it.

## Healthcare / pharma / patient education

- **Typical docs**: discharge instructions, condition leaflets, medication guides, pre-op prep, HCP education decks.
- **Viewer**: patients and caregivers, often low health literacy, stressed, older, multilingual; or HCPs short on time.
- **What magnetic means**: "this finally makes sense and I know what to do tomorrow morning". Calm clarity beats spectacle; High still works if the drama is the body's real signal.
- **Guardrails**:
  - Never contradict, soften, or reinterpret clinical instructions. Thresholds and "call when…" lines stay in the doc's terms.
  - No blame ("if you'd just followed the rules…"). No shame about weight, diet, adherence.
  - No invented outcomes or mortality numbers. Use the doc's numbers or `[ADD SOURCED STAT]`.
  - Include "your care team" as an ally; calling is normal, expected, not a failure.
  - Flag in Engine notes anything that needs clinical review (e.g. the engine added a mechanism explanation the doc doesn't state).
- **Common failures**: Transfer Failure (knows the rule, misses the moment), False Certainty (nodded at discharge, can't explain why).

## Corporate L&D / compliance / onboarding

- **Typical docs**: policies, SOPs, handbooks, product manuals, safety procedures.
- **Viewer**: employees who were assigned this; skeptical of training; know the "right answer" but act on habit.
- **What magnetic means**: "that's actually my Tuesday" — recognisable workplace moments, a bit of wit, no corporate voice.
- **Guardrails**: legal/policy wording that defines obligations stays exact; don't imply consequences the policy doesn't state; no mocking coworkers or roles.
- **Common failures**: Transfer Failure (knows policy, doesn't act under pressure), Cognitive Overload (40-page policy, no hierarchy).

## Publishing / education

- **Typical docs**: textbook chapters, course notes, ebooks, study guides, non-fiction excerpts.
- **Viewer**: students or curious adults; may be studying for an exam; used to creator-style YouTube.
- **What magnetic means**: "I get it now, and I want the next one". Curiosity and mental models; High can be very creator-like.
- **Guardrails**: accuracy to the source; don't oversimplify into wrongness; keep the author's claims attributed as theirs if contestable.
- **Common failures**: Concept Fragmentation (memorised pieces, no model), False Certainty.

## NGO / mission-driven

- **Typical docs**: impact reports, program briefs, advocacy papers, research summaries.
- **Viewer**: public, donors, partners, policymakers; emotionally saturated, skeptical of guilt-trips.
- **What magnetic means**: "I see the system, and there's something I can do". Stakes story + clear ask.
- **Guardrails**: no poverty/suffering spectacle; no invented beneficiary stories — use the doc's, or frame a composite clearly as "imagine"; dignity-first language; the ask must match what the org actually asks for.
- **Common failures**: Transfer Failure (cares, doesn't act), Fragmentation (sees tragedy, not cause).

## Unclear ICP

Default to the lens whose guardrails are strictest among the plausible options, and say which you assumed.
