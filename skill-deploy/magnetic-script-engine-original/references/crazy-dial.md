# The crazy dial

The slider is the product's main character. On the same doc, moving it must produce a difference a prospect can *feel* in the first sentence. If Low and High hooks read like the same sentence with one adjective swapped, the dial is broken.

## What the dial changes

| Dimension | Low — keep it safe | Medium — balanced, a little spicy | High — scroll-stopping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promise to viewer | "You'll understand this clearly." | "This is more interesting than you'd think." | "You can't look away from this." |
| Hook move (bias) | The moment, calm; or a clear question | The wrong belief; the number that doesn't look scary; the contradiction | The dare; a sharp cold open mid-scene; a confession; the contradiction at full volume |
| First line | Orients the viewer | Surprises the viewer | Grabs the viewer |
| Sentence temperature | Warm, steady, reassuring | Conversational, some punch, light wit | Punchy fragments, rhythm shifts, attitude, bold claims (true ones) |
| Pacing | Even beats, room to breathe | Varied beats, a few quick cuts | Fast beats, hard cuts, micro-cliffhangers |
| Stakes framing | Stated plainly, once | Made concrete through a scene | Dramatized — but only with the doc's real stakes |
| Humor | None or very gentle | Light, situational | Bold, self-aware (never at the viewer's expense) |
| POV / structure risk | Straightforward teaching order | One twist (reveal, reversal) | Unconventional: countdown, second-person dare, POV of an object, fake-out opening |
| Direct address | "you" as care | "you" as conversation | "you" as challenge |
| On-screen energy | Clean, calm reveals | Some kinetic emphasis | Big type hits, fast reveals, visual jokes |

## What the dial NEVER changes

- Locked facts (numbers, thresholds, legal/clinical wording). Identical across levels.
- The one job and the action trigger.
- Accuracy of the mechanism. High can dramatize a true mechanism; it can't exaggerate it.
- No invented statistics, deaths, anecdotes, testimonials, or "doctors hate this".
- No shaming the viewer (their habits, body, intelligence).
- No fear without an action attached.
- Respect for ICP guardrails in `icp-lenses.md` (e.g. patient content never discourages following clinical advice or implies the patient is to blame).

A good High script feels like a great creator made it. A bad High script feels like clickbait. The difference: the hook's promise is honest and paid off.

## Same-doc calibration example (heart failure discharge doc)

Use these to sense the gap between levels — don't reuse the lines.

- **Low:** "You're home from the hospital. Here's the one daily habit that helps you and your care team catch problems early."
- **Medium:** "The most important medical device for your heart isn't in the hospital. It's on your bathroom floor."
- **High:** "Two pounds. Overnight. Your body just sent you a warning — and most people step right off the scale and ignore it."

Notice: all three point at the same job (daily weight → call on a jump). Only temperature changed.

## Level × style interactions

- Myth-buster at Low: frame as "a common question" rather than "a myth you believe".
- First-person journey at High: allow present-tense, fragmentary inner voice; keep the clinical content spoken plainly at the moment it matters.
- Countdown / listicle at High: countdown order must still end on the most important item.
- Any style at Low for healthcare: include one reassurance line ("this is manageable", "your team expects your call") where the doc supports it.
